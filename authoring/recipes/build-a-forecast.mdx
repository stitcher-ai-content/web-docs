## What this does, and why

[Forecasting](/pipeline/build/events) extends an Indicator/KPI forward using its own history and settings alone — no events required. This guide walks through enabling that base forecast, then optionally connecting named events to sharpen the projection with known business changes.

<Note>
  This assumes a Time series indicator already exists (see [How to: Generate an artifact](/authoring/recipes/generate-an-artifact)). This guide continues from the `effective_cost_by_team` indicator built there.
</Note>

## Enable forecast on the indicator

Forecast settings live in the indicator's **Time settings** step, alongside date range and granularity.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on Enable forecast">
    Under **Date range**, check **Enable forecast**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the forecast period">
    Choose how far into the future the forecast extends — **One month**, **Three months**, **Six months**, or **One year**. The forecasted points are appended to the indicator after the last available date.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the look-back period">
    Choose how much history trains the forecast — **Three months**, **Six months**, **One year**, or **All available data**. A longer period captures more seasonal patterns; a shorter one is more reactive to recent trends.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally set a look-back cutoff date">
    An explicit start date for the training data, overriding the look-back period — useful to exclude data from before a known infrastructure change or cost restructuring.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally exclude partial months">
    Turn on **Exclude partial month from training data** and set the **Minimum look_back months required** it depends on.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The forecast is complete at this point — it projects forward from history alone.

## Customize the forecast with events

To sharpen the forecast with known business changes — a migration, a price change, a planned cutover — connect one or more event banks.

Event banks live under **Build → Events**. Add one under **Custom** and give it an **Event bank name** — the name the indicator's Event banks to apply field references.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Describe it in chat">
    Tell the assistant:

    - **What** changes — for example, *"2% increase month over month in compute cost."*
    - **When** it starts and stops — a date range, or a start date with no end.
    - **Where** (optional) — which dimensions it affects, offered as the filter columns available on the indicator (for example `BillingAccountId`, `ChargeCategory`, `ProviderName`, `ServiceName`).

    The assistant saves it as an event in the bank.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Add it directly">
    Add an item to the bank's **Events** list and set its **Event name** and **Description**. Use **Active** to control whether it currently applies; reorder with the up/down controls or remove it with delete.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Back on the indicator, set **Event banks to apply** to the bank(s) this forecast should account for.

## Review correlated costs

When an event is scoped to part of the model (a `ServiceCategory`, a `ProviderName`, a `BillingAccountId`), StitcherAI shows the other cost series that have historically moved with it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Preview the event">
    After saving a scoped event, open its preview. The **Correlated costs** panel appears beside it, ranked strongest first, with each series' direction, lead/lag, and per-dollar sensitivity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Judge which relationships matter">
    Look for a strong relationship, a direction that makes business sense, and a sensitivity large enough to move your forecast. Treat the list as a ranking to guide you, not a rule.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally add a linked effect">
    Choose **Add linked effect** on a correlated series. It is saved as an extra scoped event in the same bank, so toggling the driver's bank toggles the linked effect with it. Add linked effects one at a time; repeat for each series that should follow the driver.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Compare what-if scenarios

<Steps>
  <Step title="Toggle an event">
    Turn an event's **Active** checkbox off to simulate without it, on to include it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or toggle a whole bank">
    Add or remove a bank from the indicator's **Event banks to apply** to compare futures at the bank level — *with the migration* versus *without*.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Re-run and compare">
    Re-run the pipeline (see [How to: Execute a pipeline](/getting-started/execute-a-pipeline)) — the regenerated forecast is shown [with vs without its events](/pipeline/build/events), so the effect of what you toggled is visible directly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Worked example — forecasting effective cost by team through a migration

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enable forecast">
    On the `effective_cost_by_team` indicator: Enable forecast on; forecast period **Six months**; look-back period **One year**; no cutoff date needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build an event bank">
    A Custom bank named `Migration scenarios`. In chat: what — "increase compute cost during the migration"; when — March 1, 2026 through June 30, 2026; where — `ServiceCategory` = Compute. Saved as event `Compute migration, Mar-Jun 2026`, Active on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply it">
    Set the indicator's Event banks to apply to `Migration scenarios`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review correlated costs">
    The event's preview lists the series that historically move with Compute — say, a storage series that rises with it a month later. Add a linked effect on it so the migration's knock-on cost is in the same bank.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare">
    Toggle the event's Active checkbox off, re-run, and compare against the run with it on — the difference is exactly the migration's projected impact.
  </Step>
</Steps>
