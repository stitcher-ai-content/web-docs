In your AI tool, you describe a change and the **FinOps Agent** simulates its effect on spend from your live FOCUS model — a **baseline summary**, the **scenario's trajectory** over the horizon you choose, and a **critic review** of the decision — in one conversation.

This is the quick, agent-driven tier of what-if. The durable, versioned tier is in-product event forecasting under [Events](/build/events); the two are compared [below](#two-tiers-of-what-if).

<Note>
Available through the [Reasoning Engine](/reasoning-engine) in any MCP-supported AI platform — see [How to: Connect your AI tools (MCP)](/how-to/connect-via-mcp).
</Note>

## How a simulation runs

<Steps>
  <Step title="Baseline summary">
    The agent summarizes current spend from your FOCUS model — by service and by category — so the scenario starts from your real numbers, not a guess.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scenario">
    It applies the change you described over the horizon you asked for.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Critic review">
    Two paired critics review the scenario before you see a recommendation (details below). Both verdicts are shown.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scenario trajectory">
    The projected month-by-month path versus the baseline, so you can see when and how much the change lands.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The critic review

Every simulation is reviewed from two lenses before it reaches you. The two are meant to disagree when the decision is genuinely contested — that disagreement is the signal.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Finance critic" icon="scale-balanced">
    Should the business say yes to this spend? Cost minimization, margin impact, and contract-renewal risk — including whether a long horizon assumes today's pricing holds.
  </Card>
  <Card title="FinOps critic" icon="gears">
    Is it operationally sound? Redundancy and resilience across vendors and regions, migration cost, quality and latency trade-offs, and whether existing commitments go to waste.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Info>
The critics are a review aid, not a substitute for your approval. Each verdict lists its concerns so you can weigh them; nothing is committed on your behalf.
</Info>

## Two tiers of what-if

StitcherAI offers what-if analysis in two places. Use the one that matches the decision.

| | In-product event forecasting | Agent simulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Where** | [Build → Events](/build/events), on an Indicator/KPI, in the StitcherAI Editor | The FinOps Agent in your AI tool |
| **Mechanism** | Named **event banks** applied to a real indicator, shown with vs without events | Scenarios over the live FOCUS model, with a paired critic review |
| **Result** | A **durable, versioned** forecast artifact that re-runs with every pipeline run | A **conversational** answer for the decision in front of you |
| **Use it for** | Plans of record, budgets, governed forecasts you [control](/build/govern) | Quick exploration before committing capacity or a vendor change |
| **Get started** | [How to: Build a forecast](/how-to/build-a-forecast) | [How to: Connect your AI tools (MCP)](/how-to/connect-via-mcp) |

<Tip>
Simulation results are not saved as artifacts. When a scenario is worth keeping — the migration you've decided to do, the launch that's now on the calendar — author it as an event in [Events](/build/events) so the forecast carries it forward on every run.
</Tip>
