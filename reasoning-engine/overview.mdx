The Reasoning Engine is the always-present layer of IT-Finance agents that acts on your cost model inside your workflows. It's **agentic, not a chatbot**: a network of agents that plan and take multi-step actions against your model — with a human in the loop where it matters — rather than returning a single answer.

<Info>
The agents reason over **exactly what the Semantic Engine produced** — the same complete, normalized, attributed model (see [the two engines](/overview/two-engines)). Their plans and actions are grounded in your real numbers, not an approximation.
</Info>

## The agents

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Coordination agent" icon="route">
    Orchestrates the work and routes each request to the right agent, keeping context across a session so a multi-part task holds together.
  </Card>
  <Card title="IT Finance agent" icon="scale-balanced">
    Understands IT-finance constructs and their impacts, and reasons about a decision in those terms.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Planning agent" icon="list-check">
    Handles financial planning and forecasting, turning a goal into an ordered plan it **executes step by step with your approval**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Custom-cost agent" icon="file-import">
    Onboards arbitrary invoices and datasets into the FOCUS model — see [AI-powered ingestion](/data-foundation/ai-ingestion).
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
**Each agent is paired with a critic that reviews its work.** The critic checks the agent's reasoning and output before it reaches you, so what you see has already been challenged.
</Note>

You set StitcherAI as the **IT-Finance expert** in your AI platform, so spend-impacting decisions are reasoned against *your* model — and the agents act **under your own credentials**. An engineer or analyst doesn't leave their workflow to get grounded cost, feature, and margin context.

<Warning>
The agents run under your credentials and policies; StitcherAI does not bypass your authentication, authorization, or audit. The full trust-boundary model is covered in [Security](/security/tenancy-and-guardrails).
</Warning>

## Connect it to your AI tools

In your AI tools the Reasoning Engine appears as the **FinOps Agent**. Connecting takes a few minutes and no keys to copy:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add StitcherAI as a custom connector">
    In any **MCP-supported AI platform**, add a custom connector with the URL `https://app.stitcher.ai/v1/mcp`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign in">
    A browser window opens; sign in with your StitcherAI identity and approve the connection (standard OAuth).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select an environment and ask">
    Pick the environment to work in, then ask — cost questions, chargeback, what-if simulation, connection setup, configuration generation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
MCP access is enabled per organization by your administrator and is being rolled out organization by organization. The full walkthrough, with example asks, is [How to: Connect your AI tools (MCP)](/getting-started/connect-via-mcp).
</Note>

## Under the hood: MCP

The Reasoning Engine exposes itself as an **MCP (Model Context Protocol) server** — the open standard for connecting AI applications to external tools and data. Any MCP-compatible client can attach to it directly, which is what makes "reachable inside your own AI platforms" a protocol-level fact rather than a set of one-off integrations built and maintained per tool.

Once connected, an AI platform gets a typed catalog of capabilities to call: querying the cost model directly (with guardrails such as dry-run confirmation before a large scan), running what-if simulations, generating chargeback and submitting it to an ERP, managing data connections, and orchestrating the coordination, planning, and custom-cost agents described above. Each capability declares its own cost profile, so a lightweight lookup and a heavyweight simulation are governed — and rate-limited — differently rather than treated the same.

<Info>
Because MCP is the transport, adding StitcherAI to a new AI platform is a matter of pointing an MCP-compatible client at the server — not building a bespoke integration for every tool your teams use.
</Info>

## Orchestrating across your systems

The agents orchestrate multi-step workflows that span your systems — ERPs, ITSM, procurement, observability, and BI. The work is a sequence of actions across those tools, gated by you where it matters, not a single lookup.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Reconcile invoices">
    Pull, match, and reconcile invoices against the cost model — surfacing discrepancies rather than asking you to chase them by hand.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Run a what-if before committing capacity">
    Describe a change — grow a service, add an AI workload, swap a model — and the agent simulates it from your live FOCUS model: a baseline, the scenario's trajectory, and a **paired finance and FinOps critic review**, *before* you commit the capacity. It's the quick, conversational tier of what-if; durable, versioned scenarios live in [Events](/pipeline/build/events). See [What-if simulation with the agent](/reasoning-engine/what-if-simulation).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Generate and submit a chargeback">
    From the [allocated model](/pipeline/model/allocate), the agent reports chargeback by cost center — direct cost + allocation in − allocation out = net chargeback — drafts one invoice per cost center with a line item per provider, and submits them to your ERP. Currently in preview, with Zoho Books as the first ERP with end-to-end submission. See [Chargeback & ERP invoicing](/reasoning-engine/chargeback-and-erp).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
