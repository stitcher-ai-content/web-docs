**Contract Intelligence** treats a signed vendor agreement — a contract PDF — as a first-class source in the [cost model](/cost-model/overview). StitcherAI parses the contract into structured datasets (committed spend, term dates, rates, discounts) and models the vendor's **committed liability**: what was committed, how much has been consumed each month, the running balance, and a forward projection.

It answers three questions:

- **How much of the committed spend have we consumed?**
- **Are we on track to use it before expiry?**
- **Does the vendor's invoice match what we actually consumed?**

## Contract Intelligence vs Commitment Discounts

"Commitment" is an overloaded word. StitcherAI has two separate constructs and names them apart everywhere.

| | **Contract Intelligence** | **Commitment Discounts** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A signed vendor agreement (a SaaS, data-platform, or API provider) parsed from PDF | Cloud provider discount instruments — Reserved Instances, Savings Plans, Committed Use Discounts |
| Where the data comes from | The contract PDF, parsed into structured contract datasets | The FOCUS `CommitmentDiscount*` columns already in the billing feed |
| What you get | Committed-liability curve, Consumption, invoice reconciliation, renewal/expiry | Coverage and utilization against usage |
| Where it surfaces | The Contract intelligence source and the contract artifacts on this page | The Commitment-coverage report indicator in [Generate](/pipeline/build/generate-artifacts); see [Cloud & SaaS](/cost-model/cloud-and-saas#commitment-discounts) |

## How a contract becomes a source

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a Contract intelligence source">
    In the StitcherAI Editor, add a source of type **Contract intelligence** in Connect. Point it at the contract PDF in your file-based connection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe what to extract">
    Reference a short **parsing-instructions** note — a markdown file in your repository — that says what matters in this agreement: term, committed amounts, rates, discounts, renewal terms.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Parse into contract datasets">
    StitcherAI parses the agreement into named **contract datasets** — a cost dataset plus supporting business datasets. They appear in the schema browser under Connect → Business like any other business data.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Deriving cost when there is no billing feed

Some vendors bill only against the contract — there is no usage feed to connect. For that case you add a cost source that **derives from the contract**. It has no feed of its own; its FOCUS rows come from the parsed contract's cost dataset, under the provider name you assign.

| Billing shape | How the contract is recorded |
|---|---|
| **Up-front** | A one-time `BilledCost` when the payment is due, and a monthly `EffectiveCost` burn-down across the term |
| **Pay-as-you-go** | `BilledCost` and `EffectiveCost` recorded together each period |

The contract's discount is the difference between `ListCost` and `ContractedCost` on the committed row, so the negotiated rate is visible in the model rather than lost in the invoice total.

## Linking usage and invoices so nothing double-counts

A live usage-based cost source — a Snowflake or Twilio feed, for example — can declare a **linked contract** and a **linked invoice** in its **Links** section.

- When a usage feed is linked and active, the contract emits **no cost of its own**. It becomes intelligence-only — commitment and liability — so the same spend is never counted twice.
- The linked invoice source is what invoice reconciliation compares against Consumption.

## Consumption and reconciliation

After the linked usage feeds are normalized, StitcherAI aggregates their billed and effective cost per month and reports it **back to the contract**. That monthly figure is **Consumption** — the amount the commitment draws down by.

- An in-progress month is shown as **plan** and replaced by the **measured actual** once the month completes.
- Consumption also feeds a forward forecast of contract usage, so the burn-down projects whether the commitment will be used before expiry. See [Events & forecasting](/pipeline/build/events).

<Note>
**Consumption** is the working name for the drawn-down figure and its report; it is used consistently across the editor, the artifacts, and chat.
</Note>

## The contract artifacts

Contract artifacts are built in [Generate](/pipeline/build/generate-artifacts) like any other artifact.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Commitment burn-down" icon="chart-line">
    Publishes the liability curve per contract: committed plan, Consumption, running balance, and forecast projection.

    | Option | What it does |
    |---|---|
    | Contract selection | Which contract sources to include |
    | Include contracts without cost | Show contracts that have no linked or derived cost yet |
    | Month range | Full term by default, or a window |
    | Include forecast | Add the forward projection |

    **Currently in preview.**
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Renewal/expiry tracker" icon="calendar-clock">
    One row per contract: term end, days remaining, and an expiry state from thresholds you set (for example *expiring within 90 days*).

    **Currently in preview.**
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Invoice reconciliation" icon="scale-balanced">
    Compares each invoice-linked source's invoiced cost against its Consumption for the billing month, for all invoice-linked sources or a named subset.

    **Configurable in the editor today; the reconciliation results are coming soon.**
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Further contract artifacts — invoice audit, budget variance, and anomaly detection — are planned.

## The contracts report in chat

In the editor's chat, and through the [FinOps Agent](/reasoning-engine/overview) in your AI tools, a **contracts report** renders inline: each contract's committed spend, Consumption to date, remaining balance, and time to expiry — the same figures the burn-down artifact publishes. Ask about a contract and the report answers with the current position.

## The doc-organizer on-ramp

Getting contract and invoice PDFs into a file-based source is a filing job. The **doc-organizer** is a small local utility, delivered as a desktop extension for MCP-supported AI desktop apps, that does the filing for you and hands you the sync command.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check status">
    Confirm the source folder and the destination root the organizer will file into.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scan">
    The organizer reads each PDF and identifies the **vendor and period from the content**, not the filename.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Plan">
    It proposes a filing plan: `contracts/<vendor>/<year>/<month>/` and `invoices/<vendor>/<year>/<month>/`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply">
    Copies are filed into the plan's folders. The originals are left in place, and the last apply can be undone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sync">
    It prints the sync command (for example an S3 sync) that copies the organized folders to the landing bucket your file-based StitcherAI connection reads.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
The doc-organizer works on copies, handles PDFs only, and sends nothing over the network. Once the folders are in your bucket, a Contract intelligence source or a custom cost (PDF) source picks them up on the next run.
</Info>
