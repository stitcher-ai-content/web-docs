title: "Events"
description: "Define named business events that feed forecasts — explainable with vs without events, comparable as what-if scenarios, and propagated through the cost series that move together."
---

**Events** define named **event banks** — collections of described business changes and what-ifs (a launch, a migration, a contract change, a past anomaly) that **feed forecasts**. Toggling an event bank on or off lets you compare what-if scenarios side by side.

A forecast is a [generated artifact](/pipeline/build/generate-artifacts) built on an **Indicator/KPI**. You start from a KPI you already track — cost by team, cost per inference, a margin — and the forecast projects that same metric into the future. Anything you can measure, you can forecast.

<Note>
Looking for the hands-on version instead? [How to: Build a forecast](/authoring/recipes/build-a-forecast) walks through enabling forecast on an indicator and building the events that drive it.
</Note>

## Business-driver and event-driven

A StitcherAI forecast reflects what the business is about to do, not just where the line has been. Instead of extrapolating a trend, it incorporates **events**:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Known future changes" icon="calendar-plus">
    Launches, migrations, contract changes — what-ifs you already know are coming.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Past anomalies" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    A one-off spike you don't want repeated in the projection.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
Events are organized into named **event banks** — a collection of events you can switch on or off as a set. Each event is **authored in plain, natural language**: you describe the change in words, and StitcherAI accounts for it in the forecast.
</Note>

## Explainable

Because the inputs are named, described business events, a forecast explains itself. It's shown **with vs without its events** — two lines, so you can see exactly what the events moved — alongside a plain-language **impact summary** of what changed and why.

## What-if scenarios

<Steps>
  <Step title="Toggle an event bank">
    Switch a bank on or off — *with the migration* versus *without*, *with the launch* versus *without*.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare the projections">
    Watch the forecast respond, and compare the futures side by side.
  </Step>
</Steps>

What-if analysis is simply toggling which events apply.

<Tip>
For a quick, conversational what-if — scale a service, swap an AI model, add a workload — without authoring an event, ask the **FinOps Agent**. See [What-if simulation](/reasoning-engine/what-if-simulation). Use event banks when you want a durable, versioned scenario that regenerates on every run.
</Tip>

## Correlated costs

Cost series move together. When you scope an event to part of the model — a service category, a provider, an account — StitcherAI surfaces the **other cost series that have historically moved with it** in a **Correlated costs** panel beside the event, so one driver can propagate through the forecast.

| The panel shows | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strength** | How closely the series has moved with your scoped cost — ranked strongest first. |
| **Direction** | Whether the two move together or inversely, and which tends to lead. |
| **Lead / lag** | Whether the correlated series tends to move before, with, or after the scoped series. |
| **Per-dollar sensitivity** | Roughly how much the series has moved for every dollar the scoped series moved — a quick materiality check. |

From the panel you can **add a linked effect**: a matching change on a correlated series, folded in as an extra scoped event. Linked effects are added **one at a time** — chain them when several series should follow the same driver.

<Note>
Relationships are computed from your own cost history and are a **ranking to guide you**, not a guarantee. Review the direction and lag before adding a linked effect.
</Note>

Beyond cost history, a forecast can draw on further signals:

## What's still maturing

<Info>
**Available today:** natural-language events, event banks, with-vs-without projections, impact summaries, and correlated-cost surfacing with linked effects. **Still maturing**, and we'd rather be plain about it: the depth of **customer-authored event logic**, **external business-driver series** (headcount, customer count, transaction volume) as forecast inputs, and **confidence bands** on the projection.
</Info>
